Collecting Club and Team Dues Without Chasing People

A calm, repeatable routine for volunteer treasurers of book clubs, rec teams, running groups and PTA committees.

PayPilot EditorialOctober 12, 20266 min read

Somebody volunteered you. Maybe it was the softball captain, maybe the book club host, maybe you raised your hand at the PTA meeting because the silence had gone on too long. Either way, you are now the treasurer, and the first round of dues is due in two weeks.

The role is rarely hard because of the arithmetic. It gets hard when twelve people pay in four different ways, three forget entirely, one is going through a rough stretch, and nobody, you included, can say for certain who is square. A little structure at the start turns the job from a running chore into something closer to twenty minutes a month.

Quick takeaways

  • Publish one amount, one due date and a short cost breakdown in a single announcement.
  • Send individual Wallet requests early and log each payment the moment it arrives.
  • Remind the group without names first, then follow up privately and offer installments.
  • Keep club funds separate, report totals each season and hand over complete records.

Settle the number and the date before anyone pays

Dues land better when members can see where the figure came from. Begin with the costs you already know for the season or year, such as field rental, a league entry fee, room hire, race bibs or the shared reading list, then divide by the roster you realistically expect. Round up a little to leave a cushion for surprises, and say openly that you did.

Choose one due date rather than a loose window. Put the amount, the date and a one-line breakdown in a single announcement so nobody has to assemble the details from three separate messages.

  • The total amount and what it pays for, in a line or two
  • The due date, written as an actual day instead of 'soon'
  • Whether dues can be paid in installments
  • Who to contact privately if the amount is a problem
  • What happens to any money left at the end of the season

Send requests that explain themselves

Once the announcement is out, send each member an individual request through PayPilot Wallet instead of trusting everyone to remember to pay you. A request waits in their app as something to act on, and money sent from one PayPilot user to another can arrive within seconds, so your records can be updated almost as soon as someone taps to pay.

Whether a member can use PayPilot Wallet, and with which limits and features, hinges on their eligibility and identity verification; any applicable fees or limits appear in the app itself. Members who are new to it may need to finish setting up, so send requests a week or so before the deadline rather than the night before.

  1. Build a roster with each member's name and the contact they use in the app.
  2. Create one request per member for the exact dues amount.
  3. Put the group, the season and the deadline in the note, such as 'Tuesday Run Club, fall dues, due Oct 31'.
  4. Send them all in one sitting so everyone receives the ask together.
  5. Record the send date beside each name in your tracker.

A paid and unpaid tracker you will actually maintain

One sheet or note with a row per member outlasts any elaborate system. Columns for name, amount owed, amount received, date received and a brief notes field cover nearly everything a volunteer treasurer will need.

Update it the moment money arrives rather than at the end of the week. Your Wallet activity is the evidence; the tracker is the summary you glance at before practice. As a purely hypothetical example, picture a rec volleyball team of ten, each owing 60 dollars toward a 600 dollar league fee. Seven rows show 60 received, one shows 30 on an installment plan and two are blank. In a few seconds you know 150 dollars is outstanding and exactly which three people to contact.

Reminders that never sound like a collections notice

Plan your nudges before the first request goes out, so they read as routine rather than a reaction to someone in particular. A single group message a few days ahead of the deadline, naming no one, catches most of the forgetful: a line saying dues close Friday, with thanks to everyone who has already paid.

After the deadline passes, move to individual messages. Keep them brief, state the amount once and offer an easy path, such as asking whether splitting it into two payments would help. Resist listing unpaid members in the group chat or at a meeting. It can quietly push people out of a group they enjoy.

Handling hardship and partial payments with discretion

Sooner or later every club has a member who wants to stay but cannot pay the full amount this round. Agree on a policy with the other organizers before it comes up, so you are applying a shared rule rather than making a personal judgment about a friend.

Whatever you arrange stays between you and that member. Write 'payment plan' in your notes instead of the reason behind it, which keeps the record useful to a future treasurer without exposing anyone's circumstances.

  • Installments: two or three smaller requests on set dates
  • A reduced rate, funded by optional extra contributions if the group has agreed to that in principle
  • Contributing time instead, such as running the snack table or hauling equipment
  • A quiet deferral to next season, recorded in the tracker

Keep club money apart from your own

Nothing erodes a group's confidence faster than club funds blending into a treasurer's personal spending. Even when every dollar is accounted for, paying the field rental out of the same balance as your groceries raises questions nobody wants to voice.

Hold dues in a balance or account reserved for the group, and pay group costs directly from it. Photograph every receipt and file it by month. If you ever have to cover something personally, repay yourself with a clearly labeled transfer and keep the receipt alongside it. Whether a larger club, a PTA or a registered organization needs its own bank account, tax filings or formal bookkeeping depends on its legal status, so check with an accountant or attorney once the money grows beyond pocket-sized.

Reports to members and a clean handover

Members do not need elaborate statements, but they deserve a regular one. Once a season, share a short summary: total dues collected, spending by category, the balance carried forward and any known costs ahead. Group totals tell the story; who paid what, and when, belongs only in your private tracker.

Volunteer treasurer roles change hands often, and handovers are where records tend to vanish. Before stepping down, give your successor the tracker, the receipt folder, a list of recurring costs with their due dates, any open payment plans and the current balance with a matching screenshot or statement. Move the funds with a transfer you both confirm, and check the recipient carefully first, because a person-to-person transfer, once sent, may not be possible to undo.

FAQ

How much should club or team dues be?

Work from what the group genuinely spends: venue or field fees, equipment, league entry, refreshments and a modest cushion for the unexpected. Divide by the number of members you realistically expect, not the most hopeful roster. Share the breakdown with the announcement. People accept a figure far more readily when they can see it covers real costs rather than a number someone chose.

What should a treasurer do if a member won't pay dues?

Start with a private conversation, since a refusal often hides a misunderstanding or a money worry. Restate what dues pay for and offer installments if affordability is the issue. If the person simply chooses not to pay, point to the rule the group agreed on, such as dues being required to play league games, and let the organizers apply it consistently together.

Can a treasurer use a personal payment account for club dues?

Many small, informal groups do, but keep the club's money in a balance used for nothing else and log every movement in and out. As funds or activities grow, an account set up in the organization's own name is usually cleaner. Whether that becomes a requirement depends on the group's legal and tax status, which an accountant can help you sort out.

What if I sent a dues refund or transfer to the wrong person?

A completed person-to-person payment may not be something you can pull back. Message whoever received it right away, describe the error, request that they send it on or back to the club, and contact support if they go quiet. To prevent it, confirm the name and contact shown on screen before every transfer, especially when two members share a first name.

Do clubs have to pay taxes on the dues they collect?

That depends on how the group is organized, where it operates and how much it handles. A casual book club sharing costs is a very different situation from a PTA or a league registered as an organization. This article offers general information only, so speak with a tax professional or attorney about your group's circumstances before assuming either way.
